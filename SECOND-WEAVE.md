## PART ONE — CALEA / LAWFUL-INTERCEPT CLOSURE


> *"Quis custodiet ipsos custodes?"* — "Who shall watch the watchmen
> themselves?" — Juvenal, *Satires*, VI [VERIFIED FACT — public domain]


## HYPOTHESIS

A lawful-intercept design can stand in the same house as the no-logging
constitution [SCRIBE] — but only if three things hold at once: (1) the
company keeps **nothing to surrender** — Art. III §1: no record of user
activity: no browsing history, no DNS query logs, no location trails, no
ledgers of persons [DICTATION]; (2) the company maintains **intercept
capability only where the law actually requires it** — the capability
demanded by the Communications Assistance for Law Enforcement Act, 47 U.S.C.
§§ 1001–1010 [VERIFIED FACT]; (3) everything the capability is ever used
for happens **in daylight** — the hash-chained ledger doctrine (Tor +
Blockchain Architecture, §III-D) [DESIGN]: every activation recorded, every
order cited, nothing silent that the law does not forbid disclosing.

The hypothesis, without varnish: *no-logging is not the same as
no-capability.* No archive of the past, but a live subscriber's traffic
isolatable on a court order — the statute's demand for capability and the
Constitution's demand for refusal, at the same time, in the same machine
[SCRIBE].

## METHOD

Walk the statute, then the Constitution, then the gap between them — and
name the gap instead of paving over it [SCRIBE]: the statute (CALEA § 102
definitions, § 103 capability requirements and limitations — 47 U.S.C.
§§ 1001–1002 [VERIFIED FACT]); the Constitution (Art. III Privacy §§1–5
[DICTATION], Art. VI §1 [DICTATION], Art. II §3 [DESIGN]); the design
position (minimize the interceptable surface, comply only on a valid order,
challenge overreach in court, report in the open, structurally separate
what the law covers from what it does not [DESIGN]).

---

## OBSERVATION — the real tension, stated plainly

**What CALEA demands.** CALEA (Pub. L. No. 103-414, 1994; 47 U.S.C.
§§ 1001–1010) [VERIFIED FACT] requires a covered telecommunications carrier
to ensure its equipment, facilities, and services are *capable* of —
**pursuant to a court order or other lawful authorization** — (1)
expeditiously isolating and enabling the government to intercept, to the
exclusion of any other communications, the subscriber's wire and electronic
communications [VERIFIED FACT — 47 U.S.C. § 1002(a)(1)]; (2) giving access
to call-identifying information reasonably available to the carrier
[VERIFIED FACT — § 1002(a)(2)]; (3) delivering intercepted communications
and call-identifying information off-premises in a transmittable format
[VERIFIED FACT — § 1002(a)(3)]; (4) doing it unobtrusively, protecting both
non-targeted communications and the secrecy of the interception itself
[VERIFIED FACT — § 1002(a)(4)].

Note the trigger: **"pursuant to a court order or other lawful
authorization."** CALEA builds the *door*; it does not hand out the *key*
[SCRIBE] — the Constitution's warrant rule (Art. III §4) [DICTATION] and
the statute's trigger agree: nothing opens without the order.

**What the statute does NOT demand.** Two § 103(b) limitations are
load-bearing [SCRIBE]: (a) § 1002(b)(1) — the Act does not authorize law
enforcement to require any specific system design or prohibit adoption of
any feature [VERIFIED FACT]. The company may lawfully keep building
private-by-design systems; the statute refuses to be a blueprint for
surveillance engineering [SCRIBE]. (b) § 1002(b)(3) — the encryption
limitation: a carrier is not responsible for decrypting, or ensuring the
government's ability to decrypt, communications **encrypted by a subscriber
or customer**, unless the carrier provided the encryption and holds the
decryption information [VERIFIED FACT]. This is the statute's own answer to
Article III §3 — "What the company cannot read, it cannot surrender"
[DICTATION]. Where the subscriber holds the keys, the warrant is answered
with ciphertext — honestly, lawfully, in the open [SCRIBE].

**The coverage fight.** CALEA's "telecommunications carrier" definition
**excludes** entities "insofar as they are engaged in providing
**information services**" [VERIFIED FACT — 47 U.S.C. § 1001(8)(C)(i)].
**But** the FCC's CALEA Broadband Order extended CALEA to facilities-based
broadband access providers and interconnected VoIP, affirmed by the D.C.
Circuit in *American Council on Education v. FCC* (2006) [VERIFIED FACT].
So the design **assumes the ISP access line is CALEA-covered** until
counsel rules otherwise [DESIGN]. The telecom/information-service
distinction is real [VERIFIED FACT — 47 U.S.C. §§ 153(46), 153(53)]; which
TST lines sit on its sheltered side is **counsel's lane** [SCRIBE]. The DNS
registrar line — a lookup service recording key↔domain bindings in daylight
— presents a different classification question than the transmission line
[DESIGN; see Question 2].

**Pen registers and the location line.** CALEA § 103(a)(2)(B) contemplates
call-identifying information acquired under pen-register/trap-and-trace
authority (18 U.S.C. § 3127; the Pen Register Act, 18 U.S.C. §§ 3121–3127)
[VERIFIED FACT] — and bars such information from disclosing the
subscriber's physical location (beyond what the number itself reveals)
[VERIFIED FACT — 47 U.S.C. § 1002(a)(2)(B)]. The statute draws a privacy
boundary inside its own surveillance mechanism; the design discloses the
minimum the order covers and nothing adjacent [DESIGN].

**The load-bearing compromise — named, not hidden.** Where the law covers
the line, the architecture must contain a **technical isolation point** —
one subscriber's communications isolated to the exclusion of all others,
deliverable off-premises [DESIGN] — § 1002(a)(1) restated as engineering
[SCRIBE]. Article VI §1 — "An order to betray the users is refused"
[DICTATION] — does not refuse this: a valid, particularized court order is
the law's own door, and Article III §4 already opens that door on the
warrant [DICTATION]. The Constitution refuses the **voluntary**, the
**overbroad**, and the **silent** intercept [SCRIBE]. Hold all three
refusals while honoring the lawful order — that is the whole of the closure
[DESIGN].

---

## RESULT — THE DESIGN POSITION

**P1. Minimize the interceptable surface architecturally [DESIGN].** The
isolation point the law requires is built as small as the law allows, and
everything around it is built so there is nothing else to take:
end-to-end encryption with subscriber-held keys wherever the company
provides communications features (the warrant returns ciphertext, per the
§ 1002(b)(3) limitation); no plaintext at rest — loopback-only backends,
no access logs on the onion front (Tor + Blockchain Architecture, §II-C) —
the no-logging constitution as architecture, not policy; onion-first routing
as a design fact (encrypted cells at the PoP, not content; the company
holds no keys but its own services'). The isolation point itself is
**ledger-visible**: every activation a chained entry — order cited,
subscriber isolated, scope, authorizing hand, time. Where an order lawfully
gags disclosure, the entry is recorded and sealed per the order's terms —
the record exists; disclosure waits on counsel [DESIGN; the gag's scope is
counsel's lane]. *Quis custodiet* — the ledger watches the watchmen.

**P2. Warrant-only compliance; never voluntary [DESIGN].** No disclosure
without a court order or other lawful authorization — the statute's trigger
phrase [VERIFIED FACT — 47 U.S.C. § 1002(a)] and Article III §4
[DICTATION], two voices, one rule. Informal requests and fishing
expeditions are refused in writing, on the record. The user is told of the
order unless the order lawfully forbids the telling; a forbidding the
company cannot fight in the open, it fights in court (Art. III §4)
[DICTATION]. Voluntary disclosure is a breach of the fiduciary duty (Art.
I), not a kindness [SCRIBE].

**P3. Challenge overreach in court before obeying in silence [DESIGN].**
Article III §5 — "The company says no in court before it says yes in
secret" [DICTATION] — is standing procedure: dragnet orders, orders
reaching past the subscriber named, bulk call-identifying-information
demands, and orders conscripting the company into building new surveillance
features (which § 1002(b)(1) does not authorize law enforcement to require
[VERIFIED FACT]) are challenged, and the challenge is published to the
extent the law permits — Article II §3's publicity principle [DESIGN].
Scope, fightability, and appealability of any given order are **counsel's
lane** [SCRIBE].

**P4. Transparency reporting — the canary and the count [DESIGN].**
A signed, periodically refreshed **warrant canary**: the company states it
has received no secret orders it is forbidden to disclose; removal or
non-refresh signals receipt. Canaries are established industry practice
[SCRIBE]; their legal force against a gag is untested and **counsel's
lane**. Plus **aggregate disclosure counts** per period — orders received,
complied with, challenged, by type — to the granularity the law permits
[DESIGN]: Article V's honesty [DICTATION] extended into the intercept
domain.

**P5. Structural separation of service lines [DESIGN].** CALEA reaches
carriers, not everything a carrier's family touches: the access/transmission
line (facilities-based broadband) is assumed CALEA-covered and the
isolation point lives there and only there; the DNS registrar line's
classification is an open question for counsel (Question 2); portal and
information-service features are segregated from the intercept architecture,
their records — if any exist — governed by P2 and Article III §2 data
minimization; billing records (kept because the bill requires them, Art.
III §2 [DICTATION]) await counsel's ruling on which count as
"call-identifying information reasonably available to the carrier" under
§ 1002(a)(2) [SCRIBE; counsel's lane].

## QUESTIONS COUNSEL MUST RULE (numbered)

1. **Line classification.** Which TST service lines are a CALEA-covered
   "telecommunications carrier" under 47 U.S.C. § 1001(8) — each ruled
   separately, none assumed [SCRIBE].
2. **The information-service exclusion.** Does § 1001(8)(C)(i) shelter any
   TST line, and does the FCC's CALEA Broadband Order foreclose that
   shelter for the access line?
3. **Retention vs. no-logging.** Confirm no affirmative retention duty
   attaches, and rule on any federal or state data-retention regime that
   might purport to.
4. **Pen-register scope.** The reach of § 1002(a)(2)(B)'s obligation per
   covered line, including the statutory location bar and "reasonably
   available to the carrier."
5. **State regimes.** State wiretap/interception statutes, state
   data-retention or mini-CALEA proposals, state registrar obligations —
   mapped state by state before first customer.
6. **Canary, counts, and gags.** Whether P4's canary and aggregate
   reporting survive delayed-notice orders, nondisclosure orders, and NSL
   gags — and what may be published while a gag runs.
7. **Foreign process.** Posture toward non-U.S. orders, MLAT requests, and
   foreign data demands: which honored, which refused, who decides.
8. **"Reasonably achievable."** CALEA § 109 (47 U.S.C. § 1008) lets a
   carrier petition the FCC where compliance is not reasonably achievable
   [VERIFIED FACT]; whether and when TST invokes it, and cost recovery for
   compelled assistance.
